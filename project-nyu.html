Serpens Fodere

Serpens Fodere is our NYU Tandon semester-long design project: a tendon-driven robotic mechanism conceived for search-and-rescue work in rubble and confined spaces. I served as design lead in a four-person team, alongside dedicated code, circuitry, and project-management leads.

The project poster, dated April 16, 2026, presents the mechanical architecture, control concept, prototype results, and development priorities.

Modular tendon-driven mechanism

Interconnected clamping segments support the structure and guide the tendon strings. Stepper motors and spools are packaged in a compact housing at the base, where changes in tendon tension bend the segmented mechanism.

The head integrates a servo-actuated claw and an ultrasonic sensor for object manipulation and distance sensing. A stack of laser-cut plywood plates forms the base, supports the electronics, and stabilizes the assembly.

Control and integration

The poster’s control flow separates head operation from arm motion and provides commands to tension or release tendons in three directions. The circuitry diagram connects the stepper motors, motor drivers, servo, and ultrasonic sensor.

This configuration ties mechanical motion to a simple operator interface. More precise position feedback and coordinated control were identified as future improvements.

Documented prototype results

The team poster reports a maximum turn angle of 120 degrees, a maximum lifted weight of 0.61 lb, and a prototype cost of $251.18. It also plots measured tip angle against stepper-motor rotation to characterize the bending response.

These are the reported results of the classroom prototype. Navigating disaster rubble and helping recover victims were motivating applications, not field-demonstrated outcomes.

View the full project poster

Future development

The poster proposes encoders and position sensors for more repeatable motion, higher-torque motors for increased lifting capability, and a dedicated control system that coordinates motor movement with sensor feedback. These priorities address the gap between a working prototype and reliable operation under load.
