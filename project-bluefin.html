From fossil exploration to an ROV platform

The project began with a need to inspect lake beds for fossils under poor visibility. My earlier team work involved underwater exploration at Turlock Lake, collaboration with paleontology researchers, and approximately $6,000 in research grants. The BlueFin presentation documents a subsequent low-cost, modular ROV design.

The platform goal is a configurable chassis that can accept different tools and payloads. The presentation’s architecture and build progress are described below; its planned tests should not be read as completed validation.

Frame and propulsion

The design uses eight thrusters for six-degree-of-freedom motion and a frame made from 6 mm polycarbonate plates. A repeated mounting pattern allows modules to be attached without redesigning the chassis.

Each thruster uses a 2212 brushless motor and a printed housing. The thruster mounts also contribute to the frame structure, combining propulsion packaging with structural support.

Presentation: propulsion and frame, slides 3–5

Manipulator, camera, and lighting

The presentation describes a four-degree-of-freedom arm driven by five waterproof servos, mounted to the same universal interface as other modules. A compliant claw and a gesture-control module form part of the manipulation concept.

A two-servo camera assembly provides pitch and yaw motion inside a dome, with an O-ring compressed by an outer ring. The lights use two-part printed housings that retain a diffuser and are sealed with epoxy.

Presentation: arm, lights, and camera, slides 6–8

Sealing and electronics integration

The electronics tube is closed by endcaps with provisions for the tether and cable penetrations. The deck describes high-infill printed endcaps, attention to layer direction, and epoxy-sealed cable passages.

The electrical architecture brings together a Pixhawk, Raspberry Pi, ESCs, LED drivers, and power distribution. Build photographs document endcap fabrication, wiring, batteries, lighting, and cable waterproofing.

Presentation: build progress, slides 20–26

Cost target and test status

The presentation estimates a rough component total of $939, including the polycarbonate frame, motors, control electronics, batteries, lighting, tubes, and printed parts. This is the project’s estimate at the time of the deck, not a current purchasing quote.

An initial waterproof test is shown. At that stage, the next steps were to finish thruster and LED wiring, test the vehicle in water with all subsystems installed, and evaluate the seals and thrusters.
